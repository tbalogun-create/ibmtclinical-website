Why there is no "African" population

How much allele frequency varies across Africa depends on the gene — for CYP3A5 the spread is twentyfold — and where it is wide, a continental average describes nobody. A 2025 study building sub-Saharan African PBPK populations reached the same conclusion: these populations "should ideally be modelled at a regional level rather than as a single entity."

Method

Each virtual patient is assigned a genotype drawn from the cohort's observed allele frequencies, then a clearance drawn from a lognormal distribution around that genotype's typical value. Steady-state concentration follows from dose and clearance. Distributions are evaluated analytically rather than sampled, so the curves are exact for the fitted parameters.

These are two well-characterised gene–drug pairs, used to test whether the approach recovers known clinical answers. They are the validation cases for the broader model IBMT Clinical is building on African genomic and gene-expression data.
