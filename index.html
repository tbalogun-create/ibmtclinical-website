Africa carries a large share of disease but few of the trials

The continent faces a dual burden: infectious diseases such as malaria, HIV and tuberculosis, alongside rising rates of cancer and cardiovascular disease.

Too few trials means patients wait longer for new treatments, and the world's most genetically diverse populations are underrepresented in the data behind new medicines. A major reason is that regulatory approvals and site readiness are slow and hard to predict.

How it works

AI does the heavy lifting. People make the decisions.

  1. AI prepares

    Our AI maps your protocol to each country's regulatory and ethics requirements, drafts the submission package and flags gaps before they turn into queries.

  2. Experts review

    Regulatory specialists with local experience check every document before it's submitted. Nothing goes out on AI alone.

  3. Our local team delivers

    We submit and follow through to approval, then activate sites and recruit patients, with timelines you can track in one dashboard.

Founder and CEO

Toheeb is a fifth-year PhD candidate at the University of California, San Diego, where his research uses AI and computer-aided drug design to accelerate drug discovery. His work on the dynamics of a bat coronavirus is published in Biophysical Journal (Cell Press). He also brings about two years of industry drug discovery experience across small molecules and peptides, and has received multiple academic awards and fellowships.

Born and raised in Nigeria, Toheeb combines deep local knowledge with international scientific training. He is GCP-certified and founded IBMT Clinical to make Africa a predictable, high-quality place to run clinical trials, and to build lasting research capacity across the continent.
